Looped language models reuse one block of layers, so the loop count k is a compute knob. We ask whether a per-request controller can set k well for Dory1.0-4B served on an Apple M4 laptop with a pinned llama.cpp build. On GSM8K, MMLU-Pro and MBPP, holdout accuracy is flat in k: fixed k=1,2,4 score 0.590, 0.587 and 0.607. Per-item headroom is large: a per-prompt oracle reaches 0.667 at mean k 1.12. Two learned routers, a text-feature policy and a hidden-state probe, capture none of it (change versus fixed k=2: -0.003 and +0.003; intervals include zero). A two-rule heuristic found after seeing the holdout split (fixed k=1, k=2 for code) ties fixed k=2 on the 100-prompt final split at about 0.62 of its modeled latency; we label it post hoc. MMLU-Pro labels are confounded by output truncation. We reproduce the Dory authors' self-speculative decoding in llama.cpp (1.21x, identical outputs, below their reported 1.37-1.71x), and show that a sequential A/B on a shared laptop gave a spurious 0.29x slowdown that interleaving removed. Numbers follow a grader correction; the final split was scored twice and both scorings are reported. We report a negative result with its protocol decisions.
